Improving the laptop splat — what worked, what didn't, and why

1 · FLUX vs Z-Image (inpainter choice)

Masked region — original | masked | Z-Image | FLUX. FLUX continues the real material; Z-Image hallucinated an off-context blob.

Zoom (GT | Z-Image | FLUX):

→ FLUX.1-Fill is the default inpainter (FP8 on the 4090). The only dedicated trained Fill model.

2 · Generating a GENUINELY-MISSING surface (the good case)

Real photo | masked ("pretend the table bottom-half wasn't captured") | FLUX-generated continuation:

→ FLUX convincingly generates absent uniform surfaces (table/ground). This is the right use of generation.

3 · The fuzzy LID — capture-limited, NOT fixable faithfully

original | densified+re-textured (your anchors idea) | REAL photo — all three identical; the real photo itself is featureless.

→ +1.08M anchors + re-texture vs real photos = LPIPS 0.3326→0.3310 (noise). The detail isn't in the footage (Cause B). The splat already matches reality; the lid IS smooth.

4 · Below the table / legs — the hardest case

Head-on (captured) | zoomed-out (red = empty/missing below & beside the table):

Forced fill attempt — before (red = below-table) | after (FLUX): the region is cluttered with never-seen floaters; InFusion can't build thin vertical legs.

→ Legs were never captured + are thin vertical structure + cluttered by floaters → single-view inpaint can't do it. Needs re-capture or a 3D-consistent generator.
